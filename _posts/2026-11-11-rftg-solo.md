I should start by saying I'm not really the target audience for this
box. My opinion on _RftG_ can be <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2962774/the-faults-in-its-stars"
>summed up</a>:

> It's an excellent game. But it's an excellent game that I had to
> play dozens of times to appreciate. Most of those plays left me
> slightly annoyed and confused about what made so many people love
> this game so much.

I play it online from time to time, but my physical copy rarely gets
played. _Expansion and Brinkmanship_ includes three expansions that are
out of print in an affordable package compatible with the second
edition of the base game. The new systems are designed for people who
have played the base game so many times they've wrung out many of the
interesting decisions from the base game and are looking for something
more. Since these expansions are no longer available on their own,
it's great that they have been released as a bundle.

My interest is primarily the solo mode that came in the first
expansion, <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgameexpansion/34499/race-for-the-galaxy-the-gathering-storm">_The
Gathering Storm_</a>, and was itself expanded by <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgameexpansion/40210/race-for-the-galaxy-rebel-vs-imperium"
>_Rebel vs Imperium_</a> and <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgameexpansion/66121/race-for-the-galaxy-the-brink-of-war"
>_The Brink of War_</a>. Instead of being spread across three boxes,
you can now get all the official solo content in one place. (Note,
however, there are <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/filepage/248514/updated-solo-rules-for-rftg-expansion-and-brinkman"
>Updated Solo Rules for RFTG: Expansion and Brinkmanship</a> which
clarify the robot's use of the prestige mechanism added in the third
expansion.)

The core of the system is the robot's play mat and custom dice.

<img src="https://cf.geekdo-images.com/A-F6Np2t5ySRUlCF9qInhw__small/img/5hy5uj6UhqyM8xHHEX03Y-SF-Vo=/fit-in/200x150/filters:strip_icc()/pic9453232.jpg" border=0>


The play mat gives default instructions about how the robot plays each
phase. Just like a human opponent, it follows all the selected
phases. It also has two dice that have <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/image/389536/race-for-the-galaxy-the-gathering-storm"
>sides that match the symbols on the play mat</a>. When it takes those
actions, the robot gets its own version of the bonus for selecting the
phase. To be clear, the robot cheats. Just like any cardboard AI, it
can't make decisions that work as well as a human player. Instead it's
designed to simulate the <i>outcome</i> of those decisions. So instead
of playing for cards from its hand, you flip through the robot's deck
that represents its hand until you find the right type of card for the
action. That can lead to the odd situation where the robot selects
develop, but runs out of cards before finding a development card. On
the other hand, if the top card is a 5-cost development, it doesn't
expend any cards to build it. (There are special rules for 6-cost
developments which do come at a cost.)

There are different versions of the robot determined by the robot's
start world. The back of the play mat and the rulebook show which
overlay pieces to take to alter the robot's behavior.

src="https://cf.geekdo-images.com/0QCOk8Af3_PPmpcVdcPVqw__small/img/KiloXj69C6L-gW6Y8IFeN7i1Wc8=/fit-in/200x150/filters:strip_icc()/pic9453241.jpg"

The New Sparta robot uses the military strategy to conquer
worlds. Instead of flipping for worlds with back circles, it flips for
military worlds with red circles. That's what the icons mean on the
large pink tile. The small grey tile is the robot's prefered action
tile, which is to take a settle action. One of the dice has two faces
with the robot icon. If that turns up, it uses the robot's preferred
action, which is to settle for New Sparta.

Note that the pink tile has a mark on the left side that looks out of
place. I originally assumed it was a printer mistake until I put the
robot tiles on the play mat.

<img src="https://cf.geekdo-images.com/EGUVlyLteC5cVkIV4X55cA__small/img/eqiEYrvxc1RJCMDEEuDqMQNATyI=/fit-in/200x150/filters:strip_icc()/pic9453244.jpg" border=0>


This indicates the level of care given to the details of the solo
mode. That mark is actually the close parenthesis of the develop
action that would be covered by this robot's settle
overlay. Completely unnecessary, but appreciated nonetheless. I also
appreciate the numbers on the tiles that match the number on the start
world.

In the above image, you can see one of the dice has the star (*)
icon. This indicates the robot will match the human selection. It's a
way for the human to influence the bot. Since you know what the robot
will try to do, you can sometimes select phases it doesn't get much
benefit from and when it randomly matches your selection, it will be
stuck doing something suboptimal. Again, the robot cheats, so you
shouldn't feel bad about exploiting it sometimes.

<img src="https://cf.geekdo-images.com/RTHAgRAfwTAjfFEXcVze5w__small/img/Dv-DqpPlB8nV1FDTd6BrLGIGoUA=/fit-in/200x150/filters:strip_icc()/pic9453556.jpg" border=0>


Playing the bot is fairly simple once you internalize the
symbols. (This is _Race for the Galaxy_, afterall.) Taking the robot's
actions doesn't take much effort either. Games go fairly quick and the
robot scoring is simpler than the regular scoring rules. Instead of
calculating the score of 6-cost developments, they simply provide 6
points (9 points for the hard difficulty). Again, the robot cheats
because it doesn't know how to set up synergetic strategies. Games are
close for me even on the easy difficulty.

There are plenty of robots to play against and you can slowly add in
the new cards from each expansion too. The robots use the optional
modules (goals and takeovers), but they do work with the other cards
in the expansions and the prestige rules. It's a well-implemented solo
system and worth getting even if you don't plan to play with other
people.

So that's the traditional ending of the review. I'm not quite done
yet, but I'm going to put the rest of my thoughts in a spoiler tag
because . . . I'm about to do the equivalent of revealing a magic
trick.

<details>
<summary>_Spoilers!_</summary>
Theme in games is always an illusion. Take <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/50/lost-cities" >Lost
Cities</a>, which is essentially an abstract card game, but has <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2772309/the-espresso-of-card-games"
>a theme that's effective for me</a>. After I worked out how to run
the robot, I felt like Race for the Galaxy's solo system really did
put me in a competition between galactic civilizations. I looked
forward to turning a random start card to see what sort of
civilization I would face. And the robot actions do feel very thematic
to the chosen start world.

As I played more games, however, I noticed I wasn't paying much
attention to the robot tableau. I started overlapping cards to save a
bit of space on the table, Then I got into a habit of shoving the
tableau into a stack after the first 6 cards were played so that I
could see the robot's progress at a glance. My goal began to be
building my tableau to twelve before the robot finished the
game. While the timing does vary and I haven't recorded game length,
it feels like robots that focus on building get out a card a turn or
so.  When the robot beats me, I notice how lucky it got with 6-cost
developments (or the 7-point Rebel Homeworld). And that's when I
realized that while I appreciate the clever design of the robots, I'd
probably be just as happy playing against a fixed number of turns and
a score threshold. Maybe something like <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2908813/a-game-about-applied-research"
>the Ares Expedition solo mode</a>.

Perhaps what I'm really looking for is something along the lines of
the <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2681238/race-for-the-galaxy-solo-campaigns"
>Race for the Galaxy Solo Campaigns</a> which explore different RftG
strategies. The robots were <a
href="https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1301947/article/18010316#18010316"
>carefully designed</a> to push back against experienced players by
using experienced play as a model input. This gets at my fundamental
tension with the RftG design. I'm looking to explore a vast universe
and the game system rewards deep knowledge of the cards that might
come into play.
</details>

That said, I've only opened The Gathering Storm set and I should
probably finish the rest of the robots to see what tricks they are
hiding. And maybe up my winning percentage against the easy difficulty
too. 😜
